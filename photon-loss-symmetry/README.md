# An elementary symmetry restriction on 18-photon loss codes

A two-mode code in the fixed 18-photon sector cannot encode one qubit, correct every loss pattern of up to four photons, and be invariant under tetrahedral mode rotations. The same exclusion holds for octahedral and icosahedral mode rotations, whose rotation groups contain a tetrahedral subgroup. This note gives a four-dimensional proof of that special case.

## Prior result for arbitrary codes

Giannisis Manes, Subramanian and Jiang identified 18 photons as the unresolved distance-five sector in their comparison. In a public manuscript dated 10 November 2026, Yuxuan Zhang gave a general lower bound of 19 in the equivalent symmetric-qubit model, covering arbitrary complex codewords. The lower bound uses exact certificates for a three-copy semidefinite relaxation. We reproduced the ten rational certificates for lengths 9 through 18 and checked selected recoupling and partial-time-reversal coefficients directly in the 18-photon sector. The public manuscript records that external expert review is outstanding. A 19-qubit code was already constructed by Aydin, Alekseyev and Barg. ## Reduction to four basis states

The 18-photon sector carries the spin-9 representation. The three half-turns about orthogonal axes form a Klein four group, and a tetrahedral rotation of order three permutes its three nontrivial characters. If a two-dimensional invariant code contained one nontrivial character, it would contain all three. Its vectors therefore lie in the common fixed space of the half-turns.

A half-turn about the $z$ axis acts on $|k,18-k\rangle$ by $(-1)^{k-9}$. A half-turn about the $x$ axis sends it to $-|18-k,k\rangle$. The common fixed space has the four orthonormal basis vectors

$$
u_k=\frac{|k,18-k\rangle-|18-k,k\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},
\qquad k=1,3,5,7.
$$

## Incompatible error-detection conditions

Correction of up to four losses requires scalar compression of all collective-spin polynomials of degree at most four. Normal ordering expresses each such polynomial as a linear combination of products $L^\dagger M$, where $L$ and $M$ annihilate the same number of photons, at most four. These are among the Knill–Laflamme error products. In the four basis states above, $D=J_z^2$ is diagonal with entries $x=(64,36,16,4)$. For $X=J_+^2+J_-^2$, only consecutive basis states are connected, with squared couplings $1632,4200,6552$. [Loss and tensor-rank equivalence](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00561v1#S3.SS2)

Write the isometry from the logical qubit to the putative code as a four-row matrix $W$. For each row $w_i$, put $B_i=w_i^\dagger w_i$. The $B_i$ are positive matrices of rank at most one. Scalar compression of $I,D,D^2$ says that their traceless parts satisfy three homogeneous equations with coefficients $1,x_i,x_i^2$. The Vandermonde matrix has rank three and kernel generated by $(2,-9,14,-7)$. Thus the traceless parts are multiples of one nonzero traceless Hermitian matrix, with the multipliers in that ratio. In its eigenbasis, rank one and normalization force the logical states onto supports $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,4\}$. Their squared amplitudes on these supports are $(2,14)/16$ and $(9,7)/16$, respectively.

Let $p,q,r$ be the three edge terms in the off-diagonal logical matrix element of $X$, in order along the four-state path. The compressions of $X$ and $DX+XD$ must both have zero off-diagonal entries, so

$$
p+q+r=0,\qquad 100p+52q+20r=0.
$$

This forces $|q/p|^2=25/4$. The prescribed weights and couplings instead give $|q/p|^2=14\cdot4200/(2\cdot1632)=1225/68$. Both terms are nonzero and the rational numbers are unequal, which rules out the code for any choice of phases.
